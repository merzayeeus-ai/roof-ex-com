Timing Your Replacement

Timing your roof replacement correctly can affect cost, quality, and scheduling. The Bay Area's Mediterranean climate creates distinct advantages and challenges throughout the year.

Late Spring Window

Late Spring (May-June): This is often the ideal window. Rain season is over, temperatures are mild (shingle adhesive activates properly), and crews are ramping up but not yet at peak demand. Scheduling is relatively easy.

Summer Peak Season

Summer (July-September): The busiest season for roofing. Weather is reliable but demand is highest, which can mean longer wait times and sometimes higher pricing. However, the dry conditions guarantee no weather delays.

Fall Scheduling

Fall (October-November): An excellent second window. Temperatures are still warm enough for proper installation, and demand typically drops after summer rush. The key risk is scheduling too close to the rainy season—if your project gets delayed, you could be caught in November rains.

Winter Considerations

Winter (December-March): The Bay Area's rainy season makes winter installations riskier. However, experienced crews can work between storms, and winter is when you'll find the most competitive pricing and fastest scheduling due to lower demand.

Our Recommendation

Our recommendation: schedule your roof replacement for May-June or September-October for the best combination of weather conditions, crew availability, and pricing. If you need emergency replacement during rainy season, we can accommodate it with appropriate weatherproofing between work days.

Pre-Project Inspection Process

Regardless of when you schedule, a thorough inspection process is critical to a successful roof replacement. The pre-project inspection evaluates three key areas: deck condition (checking for rot, sagging, or delamination in the plywood sheathing), ventilation adequacy (confirming soffit and ridge vent capacity meets the 1:150 or 1:300 ratio for your attic square footage), and drainage performance (assessing gutter flow, valley channeling, and downspout discharge points). In the Bay Area, homes in hillside neighborhoods like Twin Peaks, Montclair, and Saratoga Hills require additional evaluation of slope drainage and retaining wall interfaces. 3-Checkpoint Quality Methodology

During the project itself, ROOF EXPRESS follows a rigorous 3-checkpoint quality methodology. Checkpoint 1 occurs after tear-off, when a crew lead inspects the entire roof deck for structural integrity, marks any sheathing that needs replacement, and photographs conditions for your records. Checkpoint 2 happens after underlayment and flashing installation but before shingle application—this is when proper ice-and-water shield placement, valley flashing alignment, and ventilation integration are verified. Checkpoint 3 is the final quality review after all shingles, ridge caps, and trim are installed, confirming proper nail patterns, uniform courses, and clean flashing details at every penetration and wall junction.

City Inspection & Warranty Follow-Up

After your contractor's quality checks, the city final inspection completes the process. A municipal building inspector visits the site to verify that work matches the approved permit scope, that materials comply with Title 24 energy efficiency requirements, that fire-resistance ratings meet local codes, and that flashing and ventilation installations meet California Building Code standards. In most Bay Area cities, inspections are scheduled within 3-7 business days of the request. Following the city sign-off, your warranty coverage begins. ROOF EXPRESS schedules a complimentary warranty inspection at the 12-month mark to check for any settling, flashing movement, or sealant issues that may develop during the first year—especially important for homes in high-wind zones like the San Bruno Gap corridor, the Altamont Pass area, and exposed coastal ridgelines.

Key takeaways

  • May–June is the ideal window: mild temps, post-rain, lower demand than summer
  • Summer offers reliable weather but highest demand and potentially higher pricing
  • September–October provides an excellent second window before rainy season
  • Winter offers best pricing but weather risks—experienced crews work between storms
  • Pre-project inspections evaluate deck condition, ventilation adequacy, and drainage performance
  • ROOF EXPRESS uses a 3-checkpoint quality methodology: post-tear-off, post-underlayment, and final review
  • City inspectors verify Title 24 compliance, fire ratings, and code-compliant flashing and ventilation
  • A complimentary 12-month warranty inspection catches settling and sealant issues early
