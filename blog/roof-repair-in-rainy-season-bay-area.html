Rainy Season Roof Repairs

The Bay Area's rainy season (November through March) is ironically when most homeowners discover they need roof repairs. The good news: many repairs can be performed between storms, and emergency measures can protect your home until permanent repairs are possible.

What Can Be Done Now

What CAN be done during rainy season: emergency tarping (to stop active leaks immediately), interior leak mitigation (directing water to prevent interior damage), minor repairs between storms when the roof is dry (patch work, sealant application), and full roof replacements during dry windows of 3+ consecutive days.

What Should Wait

What SHOULD wait for dry weather: large-scale shingle installation (adhesive strips need warmth to activate properly), any work requiring the roof to be open for extended periods, coating applications (require dry surface and 24-48 hour cure time), and non-urgent repairs that don't pose immediate water damage risk.

Scheduling Strategy

Scheduling strategy: the Bay Area typically gets dry windows of 3-7 days between storm systems, especially in January and February. Experienced roofers watch weather patterns closely and schedule work during these windows. We maintain a priority list so when a dry window opens, we can mobilize quickly.

Temperature Considerations

Temperature considerations: most roofing adhesives and sealants require temperatures above 40°F for proper curing. Bay Area winter temperatures rarely drop below this threshold in daytime, but early morning installations in December-January may need to wait until the surface warms.

Emergency Response

If you discover a leak during rainy season, call us immediately. We provide same-day emergency tarping in most cases and can schedule permanent repairs during the next available dry window. Waiting until spring often results in significantly more interior damage and higher total repair costs.

Key takeaways

  • Many roof repairs can be performed between storms during 3–7 day dry windows
  • Emergency tarping stops active leaks immediately during rainy season
  • Large-scale shingle work and coatings should wait for dry, warm conditions
  • Waiting until spring often causes significantly more interior damage
