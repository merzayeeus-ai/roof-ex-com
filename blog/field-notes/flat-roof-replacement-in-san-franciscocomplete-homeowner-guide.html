Flat Roof Replacement in San FranciscoComplete Homeowner Guide Flat and low-slope roofs are a major part of San Francisco's residential architecture.

From the Sunset and Richmond districts to Noe Valley, Bernal Heights, Mission, Excelsior, Potrero Hill, and other neighborhoods, thousands of San Francisco homes have flat or low-slope roofing systems.

These roofs require a very different approach from conventional pitched shingle roofs.

When a flat roof begins leaking, developing open seams, showing bubbles or blisters, holding excessive water, or requiring repeated repairs, it may be time to consider a complete flat roof replacement in San Francisco.

At Roof Express, we provide professional flat and low-slope roofing services throughout San Francisco and the Bay Area, including modified bitumen roofing, multi-layer torch-down systems, roof-deck replacement, skylight work, flashing, drainage improvements, and complete reroofing.

This guide explains what San Francisco property owners should know before replacing a flat roof. Why Flat Roofs Are So Common in San Francisco San Francisco has a unique collection of homes and multi-unit residential buildings.

Many buildings are constructed on narrow lots where a flat or low-slope roof allows homeowners to maximize interior space while maintaining the building's architectural profile.

  • ●Each of these areas requires proper waterproofing.

  • ●Not every roof leak means you need a complete roof replacement.

If a relatively new roofing system has one isolated flashing problem, repair may be sufficient.

However, replacement becomes more practical when deterioration affects the roofing system as a whole.

Common warning signs include:

Recurring roof leaks

If you're repairing the roof every rainy season, a complete replacement may provide better long-term value than continuing to patch an aging system. What Is the Best Flat Roofing System for San Francisco? There is no single roofing system that is correct for every building.

One of the systems commonly used for residential low-slope roofing is modified bitumen roofing. Modified Bitumen Flat Roofing Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based roofing membrane specifically designed for low-slope applications.

Unlike conventional shingles, which depend heavily on roof slope to shed water, a modified bitumen roofing assembly is designed to provide continuous waterproof protection.

A properly installed modified bitumen system can provide:

Multiple waterproofing layers

Reinforced membranes

Strong seam protection

Durable surface protection

Effective detailing around penetrations

Compatibility with many San Francisco flat roofs

Modified bitumen is available in several systems and installation methods.

  • ●One option is a multi-layer torch-applied system.
  • ●3-Layer Torch-Down Roofing in San Francisco
  • ●For appropriate properties, Roof Express may recommend a 3-layer modified bitumen roofing system.

A typical multi-ply system can consist of:

Layer 1 – Base Sheet

Layer 2 – Modified Bitumen Interply

Layer 3 – Granulated Modified Bitumen Cap Sheet

These layers work together to create a reinforced roofing assembly.

Instead of depending on one surface membrane, the roofing system provides multiple layers of waterproofing protection.

This type of construction can be especially beneficial on low-slope roofs where water moves more slowly than it does on a steep shingle roof. How Torch-Down Roofing Works Torch-applied modified bitumen uses controlled heat during installation to bond compatible roofing membranes to the approved substrate or underlying roofing layer.

When installed correctly, the process creates bonded seams and a continuous waterproofing system.

Because torch-down roofing involves hot work and open-flame equipment, additional safety requirements can apply.

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection guidance specifically notes that roofing involving hot work, including torch-down work, is routed to the San Francisco Fire Department as part of applicable permitting review.

This is one reason torch-applied roofing should be performed only by qualified roofing professionals using appropriate equipment and safety procedures. Complete Flat Roof Replacement Process A professional flat roof replacement involves much more than installing a new membrane over the existing roof.

Here is how a complete project may be approached. 1. Detailed Roof Inspection Before construction begins, the roofing contractor should inspect the entire roof.

Important conditions include:

Existing membrane

Seams

Roof edges

Parapet walls

Skylights

Drains

Scuppers

Plumbing vents

Roof-to-wall connections

Low areas

Existing patches

Visible decking problems

The purpose of the inspection is to determine whether repair, overlay, or complete tear-off is the most appropriate approach. 2. Removal of the Existing Roofing For a complete replacement, the existing roofing material is removed from the areas included in the project.

Tear-off may include:

Existing roofing membranes

Old cap sheets

Base materials

Loose flashing

Fasteners

Previous patching materials

Deteriorated roofing accessories

Removing the old system allows the contractor to see the condition underneath. 3. Roof Deck Inspection One of the biggest advantages of a complete tear-off is that it exposes the roof decking.

Concealed damage may include:

Rotten plywood

Soft decking

Water damage

Delamination

Dry rot

Previous improper repairs

Damaged framing

A new flat roof should be installed over a sound and properly prepared substrate.

Covering severe decking deterioration with another roofing membrane does not correct the underlying problem. 4. Roof Deck Replacement Deteriorated plywood or sheathing should be replaced where necessary before the new roofing system is installed.

Homeowners should understand how decking replacement will be handled before signing a roofing contract.

A detailed proposal should explain:

Any included plywood allowance

Additional decking cost

How concealed conditions are documented

How additional work is authorized

Structural repairs that may be excluded

Clear terms can prevent surprises during construction. 5. Evaluate Roof Slope and Drainage Drainage is one of the most important parts of a San Francisco flat roof.

Despite the name, a properly functioning "flat" roof should generally have enough slope to direct water toward the intended drainage system.

Water may drain toward:

Roof drains

Scuppers

Gutters

Downspouts

If the existing roof has significant ponding, installing a new membrane alone does not necessarily fix the slope.

Depending on existing conditions, drainage improvements may require:

Tapered roofing materials

Crickets

Framing modifications

New plywood

Drain modifications

Scupper improvements

Other slope corrections

  • ●The drainage condition should be evaluated before installation whenever practical.
  • ●Why Ponding Water Matters
  • ●Some temporary water can remain on certain low-slope roofs following rain.

However, significant or persistent ponding deserves investigation.

Ponding can occur because of:

Insufficient slope

Blocked drains

Settling

Deflected framing

Poorly positioned drains

Improper previous roof construction

Standing water can place additional stress on roofing materials and expose weak seams or flashing details.

A flat roof replacement proposal should clearly state whether drainage or slope correction is included. 6. Install the Base Sheet Once the deck and substrate have been properly prepared, installation of the roofing system begins.

For a three-layer modified bitumen system, the first layer is typically the base sheet.

The base sheet creates a foundation for subsequent waterproofing membranes.

Its exact type and attachment depend on:

Manufacturer requirements

Roof substrate

Building conditions

Selected roofing assembly

Proper preparation at this stage is critical because every layer above depends on the foundation underneath. 7. Install the Modified Bitumen Interply The second layer provides another reinforced waterproofing membrane.

This intermediate roofing layer increases redundancy within the system.

Rather than relying entirely on the finished cap sheet, the roof contains another continuous layer beneath it.

This is one of the primary advantages of a multi-ply roofing assembly. 8. Install the Granulated Cap Sheet The third and final layer is commonly a granulated modified bitumen cap sheet.

This becomes the visible surface of the completed roof.

The cap sheet provides the final waterproofing layer while its granulated surface helps protect the roofing membrane from weather exposure.

The cap sheet must be properly incorporated into all important roof details.

These include:

Parapet walls

Roof drains

Scuppers

Skylights

Plumbing vents

Chimneys

Roof edges

Roof-to-wall transitions

Other penetrations

These may require custom fabrication.

If custom skylights are being replaced, they should be measured and ordered before the roofing schedule is finalized whenever possible.

That can reduce unnecessary delays and avoid installing a new roof only to disturb it again when the replacement skylight arrives. Roof Drains and Scuppers Drainage components should never be overlooked during flat roof replacement.

Simply applying roofing cement around an old drain is not equivalent to properly rebuilding the drainage detail when deterioration is present. Plumbing Vents and Other Penetrations Every hole through a roofing system creates another potential leak location.

The roofing contractor should also evaluate existing penetrations around solar mounts so the new waterproofing can be properly integrated. Roof Decks Over Flat Roofs Some San Francisco properties have decks or walking surfaces installed over roofing membranes.

A roof deck can conceal deterioration for years, making inspection especially important when leakage occurs below it. Do You Need a Permit for Flat Roof Replacement in San Francisco? A complete flat roof replacement will generally require a building permit.

Under San Francisco's current Building Code, reroofing is exempt from a building permit only when no roof sheathing is installed, repaired, or removed and the reroofed area during a 12-month period does not exceed 25% of the total roof surface.

Because a complete flat roof replacement normally involves substantially more than 25% of the roof, homeowners should expect permit requirements to apply.

Additional review may also apply depending on the particular property and scope.

For example, City guidance identifies additional considerations involving historic buildings, street-space use, and hot-work roofing. San Francisco Roof Inspections Permitted roofing work also needs to complete applicable inspections.

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection currently allows reroofing inspections for permits issued through PermitSF to be requested online through the applicant's PermitSF dashboard.

A professional roofing contractor should coordinate the applicable permit and inspection process as agreed in the roofing contract. Cool Roof Requirements Depending on the building, roofing area, project scope, roof slope, and applicable California energy standards, a flat roof replacement may also be subject to cool-roof requirements.

These rules can affect the solar reflectance and thermal characteristics of the roofing material selected.

Because applicable energy standards and exceptions can vary by project, product compliance should be verified before materials are ordered.

A roofing product should not be chosen based solely on color or appearance. How Much Does Flat Roof Replacement Cost in San Francisco? There is no single price that accurately represents every San Francisco flat roof.

The best system depends on roof slope, existing construction, drainage, roof deck, penetrations, building requirements, and budget. Modified bitumen is one commonly used low-slope roofing option. Is a 3-layer torch-down roof a good option?

A properly designed multi-ply modified bitumen system can provide strong waterproofing redundancy for appropriate low-slope applications. The exact system should be selected according to manufacturer instructions, building conditions, and applicable requirements. Do I need a permit to replace my flat roof in San Francisco?

A complete reroof generally requires a permit. The current San Francisco Building Code exempts reroofing only when no roof sheathing is installed, repaired, or removed and the work does not exceed 25% of the total roof surface within a 12-month period. Does torch-down roofing require special consideration in San Francisco?

Yes. San Francisco DBI guidance identifies torch-down roofing as hot work and notes routing to the San Francisco Fire Department where applicable. Can a new flat roof eliminate ponding water?

Not automatically. A new roofing membrane provides waterproofing but does not necessarily change the existing roof slope. Significant ponding may require separate drainage or slope improvements.

Should I replace my skylights while replacing my flat roof? Older or deteriorated skylights should be evaluated during reroofing. Replacing them at the same time can allow the new skylight and roof flashing to be integrated into the new roofing system.

What happens if rotten plywood is found? Deteriorated roof decking should be addressed before installation of the new roof. Your roofing contract should explain how concealed decking repairs will be priced and approved.

Can you install a new flat roof over an existing roof? An overlay may be possible in certain situations, but existing layers, moisture, decking condition, weight, applicable code requirements, and roofing manufacturer requirements must first be evaluated. How long does flat roof replacement take?

Many residential projects take several working days. Large roofs, difficult access, multiple existing layers, decking repairs, skylights, drainage work, weather, and inspections can extend the schedule. Does a new flat roof require maintenance?

Yes. Drains and scuppers should remain clear, and roofing membranes, penetrations, flashing, skylights, and other components should be periodically inspected. How do I get a flat roof replacement estimate in San Francisco?
