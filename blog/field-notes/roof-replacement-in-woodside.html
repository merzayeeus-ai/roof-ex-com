Complete Homeowner Guide

Replacing a roof is one of the most important investments you can make in your Woodside home.

A complete roof replacement in Woodside, CA does much more than improve curb appeal. Your roofing system protects the roof deck, framing, insulation, ceilings, walls, and everything inside your home from rain, moisture, wind, and other environmental exposure.

Woodside properties can also present unique roofing considerations. Large homes, mature trees, complicated roof designs, skylights, multiple roof elevations, valleys, chimneys, low-slope sections, solar systems, and limited access can all affect how a roofing project should be planned.

At Roof Express, we provide complete residential roofing services throughout Woodside and the San Francisco Peninsula.

Whether your home has architectural shingles, a combination of pitched and low-slope roofing, skylights, or an aging roof requiring complete tear-off, the first step is understanding the condition of the entire roofing system.

This guide explains what Woodside homeowners should know before replacing a roof.

When Is It Time to Replace Your Roof in Woodside?

Not every roofing issue requires complete replacement.

An isolated leak caused by one damaged flashing or a small area of roofing may sometimes be repaired.

However, when roofing materials become deteriorated throughout the roof or leaks continue appearing in different locations, replacement may become the more dependable long-term solution.

Common warning signs include:

Missing shingles

Cracked or curled shingles

Significant shingle granule loss

Recurring roof leaks

Water stains on ceilings or walls

Damaged or deteriorated flashing

Soft roof decking

Dry rot

Moss or organic buildup

Multiple previous roof repairs

Damaged skylight flashing

Deteriorated pipe flashings

Open roof-to-wall transitions

An older roofing system approaching the end of its expected service life

If you're unsure whether you need repair or replacement, start with a complete roof inspection.

Roof Replacement Requirements in Woodside, California

Woodside has specific reroofing requirements homeowners and contractors should understand before construction begins.

The Town of Woodside provides a dedicated re-roof permit process, and reroof permit applications can be submitted through its eTRAKiT system. The Town also requires documentation showing that proposed roofing materials meet the required roofing assembly classification.

One particularly important local requirement is that Woodside requires a minimum Class A roof assembly under its municipal requirements.

This makes product selection and the complete roofing assembly especially important.

Class A Roofing in Woodside

When choosing a new roof, homeowners should not focus only on the appearance of the shingles.

The complete roofing assembly must comply with applicable requirements.

Woodside's current reroof guidance specifically requires a minimum Class A roof. The Town's reroof documentation also includes requirements addressing roof vents, valleys, spaces beneath certain roof coverings, and gutter protection.

A professional Woodside roofing contractor should verify that the roofing materials and assembly selected for your home meet applicable Town and manufacturer requirements before ordering materials.

Gutter Guards Are an Important Woodside Requirement

Trees are one of the features that make many Woodside properties beautiful, but leaves and debris can also accumulate around roofing and drainage systems.

Woodside's reroof requirements state that roof gutters must have a means of preventing the accumulation of leaves and debris.

This is an important detail to discuss as part of the reroofing project rather than waiting until the roof is already completed.

Proper roof drainage helps move rainwater away from:

Roof edges

Valleys

Walls

Foundations

Fascia

Landscaping

Keeping gutters and downspouts functioning properly is an important part of maintaining the complete roofing system.

Roof Vent Requirements in Woodside

Roof ventilation should also be evaluated during a reroof.

Woodside's published reroof requirements contain specific provisions for new and existing exterior roof vents in areas affected by the roofing work, including requirements related to ember intrusion protection under applicable California codes.

This means replacing a roof may also be the right time to evaluate older roof vents rather than simply installing new roofing around outdated components.

What Is Included in a Complete Roof Replacement?

A professional roof replacement involves much more than installing shingles.

A complete roofing system generally includes several important stages.

1. Roof Inspection

Before construction begins, the existing roof should be evaluated.

Our inspection may include looking at:

Shingles

Roof penetrations

Valleys

Skylights

Chimneys

Flashing

Gutters

Ventilation

Low-slope roof areas

Visible decking conditions

2. Removal of Existing Roofing

For a complete tear-off roof replacement, existing roofing materials are removed down to the appropriate substrate.

This may include removal of:

Existing shingles

Roofing underlayment

Old fasteners

Damaged flashing

Roofing accessories

Existing ridge materials

Construction debris

A complete tear-off allows the roofing contractor to inspect conditions that were hidden underneath the old roofing system.

3. Roof Deck Inspection

Once the old roofing is removed, the roof decking can be properly evaluated.

This is one of the most important stages of a reroofing project.

The roof deck may reveal:

Rotten plywood

Water damage

Delaminated sheathing

Dry rot

Soft decking

Improper previous repairs

Structural deterioration

Installing a premium roofing system over damaged decking can compromise the finished project.

Deteriorated areas should be identified and corrected before the new roofing system is installed.

4. Roof Deck Repairs

When damaged decking is discovered, affected sections may need replacement.

Homeowners should understand how additional decking is handled before construction begins.

A detailed roofing proposal should explain:

Any included decking allowance

Additional plywood pricing

How concealed damage is documented

When homeowner approval is required

Whether structural repairs are excluded

Clear roofing contracts help prevent surprises once tear-off begins.

5. Roofing Underlayment

After the roof deck is prepared, new roofing underlayment is installed.

Underlayment creates an additional protective layer between the decking and finished roofing.

A complete pitched roofing system can generally be viewed as:

Roof Deck → Underlayment → Waterproofing → Flashing → Shingles → Ventilation → Drainage

Every component has an important role.

6. Waterproofing Critical Roof Areas

Some roof areas face greater water exposure than others.

Additional waterproofing protection may be appropriate around locations such as:

Valleys

Eaves

Skylights

Chimneys

Plumbing vents

Roof-to-wall connections

Other penetrations

These details are particularly important on complicated Woodside homes with multiple roof elevations.

7. Roof Valleys

Valleys collect water from multiple roof surfaces, which makes them especially important waterproofing locations.

Woodside's published reroof requirements contain specific provisions for valley flashing, including corrosion-resistant metal and underlayment requirements.

Valley construction should therefore be planned as part of the complete roofing system rather than treated as a minor finishing detail.

8. New Architectural Shingle Installation

Architectural asphalt shingles are an excellent option for many Woodside homes.

Modern architectural shingles provide:

Dimensional appearance

Numerous color choices

Strong weather protection

Manufacturer warranty options

Compatibility with many architectural styles

Good overall value

Premium shingle lines may also offer upgraded features, enhanced aesthetics, and manufacturer-backed warranty programs when eligibility requirements are met.

The correct product should be selected based on the building, roof design, applicable local requirements, and homeowner preferences.

9. Proper Roof Flashing

Flashing is one of the most important parts of the roofing system.

Many leaks occur around transitions and penetrations rather than through the middle of the roofing material.

Important flashing locations include:

Skylights

Chimneys

Plumbing vents

Sidewalls

Headwalls

Valleys

Roof-to-wall connections

Other roof penetrations

Simply installing expensive shingles cannot compensate for poorly designed or deteriorated flashing.

Skylight Replacement During a Woodside Reroof

Many Woodside homes have skylights.

Roof replacement is an excellent time to inspect them because the surrounding roofing and flashing are already being removed.

Older skylights should be checked for:

Deteriorated flashing

Failed seals

Cracked glazing

Corrosion

Rotten wood

Deteriorated curbs

Interior water staining

Previous temporary repairs

If an existing skylight is already approaching the end of its useful service life, replacing it during the reroof can prevent the need to disturb a relatively new roofing system later.

Custom skylights should be measured and ordered well in advance because replacement units may require fabrication.

What About Solar Panels?

If your Woodside home already has a photovoltaic system, it should be identified before the roofing project is scheduled.

The Town's reroof permit application specifically asks whether an existing photovoltaic system is present.

Depending on the project, solar panels may need to be removed and reinstalled by the appropriate contractor so roofing underneath them can be properly replaced.

Coordination between the roofer and solar contractor should happen before construction begins.

Flat and Low-Slope Roof Sections

Some Woodside homes contain a combination of pitched shingle roofing and flat or low-slope areas.

These sections should not automatically receive the same roofing material.

Low-slope roofs may require a system specifically designed for low-slope applications, such as a modified bitumen roofing assembly.

Areas commonly requiring low-slope roofing include:

Additions

Porches

Garage sections

Patio roofs

Connecting roof areas

Low-slope architectural sections

The transition between shingles and low-slope roofing must also be carefully waterproofed.

Why Drainage Matters

Water management is critical on any roof.

A complete roofing inspection should evaluate:

Gutters

Downspouts

Valleys

Roof slopes

Flat-roof drains

Scuppers

Areas with previous ponding

New roofing materials cannot automatically correct a major drainage or slope problem.

If drainage improvements are necessary, they should be discussed as part of the project scope.

Roof Repair or Complete Replacement?

Sometimes repairing the roof remains the right decision.

Roof Repair May Be Appropriate When:

The damage is localized

The roof is relatively young

Most roofing material remains in good condition

One flashing detail is causing the leak

Only a limited roof section is damaged

Roof Replacement May Be More Appropriate When:

Leaks occur in multiple locations

Shingles are deteriorating throughout

Significant granule loss is present

Numerous patches already exist

Decking has widespread deterioration

The roof is approaching the end of its expected service life

Repair expenses are increasing every year

A professional inspection should help homeowners understand both options whenever repair remains practical.

Frequently Asked Questions About Roof Replacement in WoodsideDoes Woodside require a reroof permit?

Yes. The Town of Woodside provides a dedicated reroof permit process, and applications can be submitted through eTRAKiT. Project-specific additional information may also be required.

Does Woodside require a Class A roof?

Yes. The Town's current reroof requirements specify a minimum Class A roofing assembly.

Are gutter guards required when reroofing in Woodside?

Woodside's reroof requirements state that gutters must have a means of preventing leaves and debris from accumulating.

What inspections are required for a Woodside reroof?

The Town's current reroof requirements list roof tear-off, plywood nailing, roof in-progress, and final inspections. The exact inspections applicable to an individual permit should always be confirmed for that project.

What roofing material is best for a Woodside home?

The answer depends on roof slope, architecture, existing construction, applicable fire requirements, and homeowner preferences. Architectural shingles work well for many pitched roofs, while modified bitumen systems may be appropriate for flat and low-slope sections.

Should I replace my skylights when replacing my roof?

Older skylights should be evaluated during reroofing. Replacing an aging skylight at the same time can allow the new flashing and roofing system to be properly integrated together.

What happens when rotten plywood is discovered?

Damaged roof decking should be corrected before new roofing is installed. A detailed contract should specify how additional plywood and concealed structural conditions are priced.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Many residential roofing projects take several working days, although large or complex Woodside homes can take longer because of roof size, access, multiple elevations, skylights, decking repairs, weather, and inspection scheduling.

Can Roof Express replace both shingle and flat roof sections?

Yes. Homes containing a combination of pitched and low-slope roofing can be evaluated so the proper roofing material is used for each roof section.

Contact Roof Express to schedule an inspection. After evaluating the existing roof, we can prepare a detailed written proposal for the recommended roofing system.
