Complete Homeowner Guide If your roof is leaking, showing its age, losing shingles, or requiring frequent repairs, it may be time to consider a roof replacement in San Jose, CA.

A roof is one of the most important components of your home. It protects the structure from rain and moisture, helps control indoor temperatures, protects insulation and framing, and contributes significantly to your property's appearance and value.

For San Jose homeowners, replacing a roof isn't simply a matter of removing old shingles and installing new ones. The condition of the roof deck, ventilation, flashing, underlayment, drainage, penetrations, skylights, local building requirements, and the type of roofing material all affect the performance of the finished roof.

At Roof Express, we help San Jose homeowners evaluate their existing roofs and choose roofing systems designed for long-term performance in Bay Area conditions.

This guide covers what homeowners should know before starting a roof replacement project in San Jose. When Should You Replace Your Roof in San Jose? Not every roofing problem requires a complete replacement.

A localized leak, damaged flashing, or several missing shingles may sometimes be repaired.

However, when deterioration becomes widespread, continuing to make small repairs can become more expensive and less reliable than replacing the roofing system.

Common signs that it may be time for a new roof include:

Missing, cracked, curled, or deteriorated shingles

Significant granule loss from asphalt shingles

Multiple roof leaks

Water staining on ceilings or walls

Soft or deteriorated roof decking

Repeated repairs in different areas

Damaged or deteriorated flashing

Roofing materials approaching the end of their expected service life

Sagging or uneven roof areas

Visible deterioration around skylights, chimneys, vents, and other penetrations

Moss or organic growth associated with prolonged moisture

An older roofing system that is becoming difficult to maintain

The best way to determine whether you need repair or replacement is to have the roof professionally inspected.

A qualified San Jose roofing contractor should evaluate the entire roofing system rather than simply looking at the area where a leak appears. What Is Included in a Professional Roof Replacement? A quality roof replacement should address the complete roofing assembly.

Depending on your home and the roofing system selected, a typical San Jose roof replacement may include: 1. Removal of the Existing Roofing Existing roof coverings are removed so the roofing contractor can inspect the underlying roof deck.

A complete tear-off also allows deteriorated underlayment, flashings, fasteners, and roofing accessories to be properly addressed. 2. Roof Deck Inspection After the old roofing material is removed, the exposed plywood or roof sheathing should be inspected.

Areas with rot, delamination, moisture damage, or structural deterioration may need to be replaced before the new roofing system is installed.

Installing premium roofing materials over deteriorated decking can compromise the entire system. 3. Roofing Underlayment Underlayment creates an additional protective layer between the roof deck and the finished roofing material.

Modern synthetic underlayments can provide improved durability and moisture resistance compared with older roofing felts. 4. Waterproofing Critical Areas Certain parts of a roof are more vulnerable to water intrusion.

Depending on the roof design, additional waterproofing may be installed around:

Valleys

Eaves

Skylights

Chimneys

Plumbing vents

Roof-to-wall intersections

Other roof penetrations

5. Flashing Roof flashing helps redirect water away from vulnerable transitions and penetrations.

Improper flashing is one of the most common sources of roof leaks, so these details should receive careful attention during a roof replacement.

The selected roofing system is installed according to the manufacturer's specifications and applicable building requirements.

Roof and attic ventilation should also be evaluated during replacement.

Does San Jose Require a Permit for Roof Replacement? Yes, in many cases.

As of 2026, the City of San José requires a building permit when 25% or more of a roof is reroofed within a 12-month period. Work involving less than 25% is generally treated as a repair rather than a reroof for this requirement.

Many reroof permits can be processed through the City's SJPermits system.

Additional review or permits may be required when a project involves circumstances such as:

Certain historical properties

Changes to rooftop equipment

Solar equipment

Structural alterations

Certain roofing materials or roof loads

Properties subject to additional planning or building requirements

For permitted reroof projects, the City also requires inspections for applicable code compliance.

Working with an experienced roofing contractor can simplify the permitting and inspection process and help ensure your new roof meets applicable requirements. How Much Does Roof Replacement Cost in San Jose? The cost of replacing a roof in San Jose varies significantly from one property to another.

Rather than relying on a generic price-per-square-foot estimate, homeowners should obtain a site-specific roofing proposal.

Factors affecting roof replacement cost include: Roof Size Larger roofs require additional material, labor, disposal, and installation time. Roof Pitch Steeper roofs generally require additional safety procedures and labor. Roof Complexity A simple roof with a few penetrations is usually less expensive to replace than a roof containing numerous valleys, dormers, walls, skylights, chimneys, and complicated transitions.

Number of Existing Roofing Layers Removing multiple existing roofing layers increases labor and disposal costs. Roof Deck Condition Deteriorated plywood or structural components discovered after tear-off can increase the project cost. Roofing Material Standard architectural shingles, premium solar-reflective shingles, tile, metal, and low-slope roofing systems all have different material and installation costs.

Skylights and Roof Penetrations Existing skylights may need to be reflashed or replaced during the reroofing process. Gutters and Drainage Some roof replacement projects also include new gutters, downspouts, drains, or drainage corrections. Permit Requirements Applicable permit and inspection costs should also be considered.

Roofing must be carefully integrated with skylight curbs and flashing systems. Replacing an aging skylight during reroofing may be more efficient than removing newly installed roofing several years later when the skylight eventually fails.

Before reroofing, inspect skylights for:

Should I repair my roof or replace it?

Repair may make sense when:

Damage is isolated

The roof is relatively young

Most of the roofing system remains in good condition

A specific flashing or penetration is causing the problem

Replacement may be more appropriate when:

The roof is approaching the end of its useful service life

Leaks occur in multiple areas

Shingles are deteriorating throughout the roof

The decking has widespread damage

Previous repairs are failing

Maintaining the existing roof is becoming increasingly expensive

A trustworthy roofing contractor should explain both options whenever repair is realistically possible. Why Proper Roof Ventilation Matters Roof replacement is also an opportunity to evaluate attic ventilation.

A properly designed ventilation system can help move excess heat and moisture out of the attic.
