Complete Homeowner Guide A roof replacement in San Francisco requires more than simply removing old roofing material and installing a new roof.

San Francisco homes present roofing contractors with unique conditions. The city has dense neighborhoods, older Victorian and Edwardian buildings, attached properties, narrow access areas, flat and low-slope roofs, skylights, rooftop decks, chimneys, parapet walls, fog, coastal moisture, wind, and complicated roof-to-wall transitions.

Because of these factors, choosing the right roofing system — and the right San Francisco roofing contractor — can make a major difference in how your new roof performs over the years.

At Roof Express, we provide professional residential roof replacement throughout San Francisco and the surrounding Bay Area. Our goal is to evaluate the entire roofing system rather than simply covering visible problems.

In this complete guide, we'll explain when to replace your roof, what a professional reroofing project should include, roofing material options, San Francisco permit considerations, flat-roof replacement, skylights, roof decking, costs, project timelines, and how to choose a qualified roofing contractor. When Should You Replace Your Roof in San Francisco? A roof does not necessarily need replacement simply because it develops one leak.

Some isolated roofing problems can be successfully repaired.

However, once deterioration becomes widespread or the roofing system reaches the later stages of its service life, continuing to perform temporary repairs can become expensive and unreliable.

Common signs that you may need a roof replacement in San Francisco include:

Repeated roof leaks

Missing or damaged shingles

Cracked, curled, or deteriorated shingles

Significant granule loss

Open seams in flat roofing

Blisters or bubbles in roofing membranes

Deteriorated roof flashing

Soft or damaged plywood

Dry rot around roof penetrations

Ponding water on low-slope roofs

Water stains on ceilings or walls

Deterioration around skylights

Damaged chimney or parapet flashing

Multiple previous roof patches

Roofing approaching the end of its useful service life

Leaks appearing in several different areas

If repairs are required every rainy season, it may be time to evaluate whether replacing the entire roofing system is more practical. Why San Francisco Roofs Are Different San Francisco is one of the most unique roofing markets in California.

Homes located only a few blocks apart can have completely different roofing systems.

You may find:

Victorian homes with steep pitched roofs

Edwardian buildings

Multi-unit residential buildings

Flat-roof homes

Low-slope roofs

Combination pitched and flat roofing

Asphalt shingle roofs

Modified bitumen roofs

Skylights and custom skylights

Rooftop decks

Parapet walls

Closely attached neighboring roofs

A roofing contractor working in San Francisco must understand how these different components interact. Coastal Moisture and Fog San Francisco's climate can expose roofs to frequent fog and moisture even when it isn't raining.

Water management is therefore extremely important.

Details around flashing, drains, scuppers, skylights, walls, chimneys, vents, and roof edges must be carefully installed. Wind Exposure Homes in exposed areas can experience significant wind.

  • ●Roofing materials must be properly fastened and installed according to manufacturer requirements.
  • ●Dense Properties
  • ●Many San Francisco homes have little space between buildings.

Some properties share roof transitions or have extremely close neighboring structures.

This means contractors need to consider:

Neighboring property protection

Debris control

Material delivery

Roof access

Driveway or street access

Scaffolding

Ladders

Disposal

Protection of windows, siding, and landscaping

  • ●Proper planning is especially important before tear-off begins.
  • ●Flat Roof Replacement in San Francisco
  • ●Flat and low-slope roofing is extremely common throughout San Francisco.

These roofs require different materials and installation techniques than standard pitched shingle roofs.

One of the biggest mistakes a homeowner can make is allowing a roofing material designed for steep-slope applications to be used in an inappropriate low-slope area. Modified Bitumen Roofing Modified bitumen systems are commonly used on San Francisco flat and low-slope residential roofs.

Depending on the particular system, installation may include multiple layers such as:

Base sheet

Reinforced membrane

Modified bitumen roofing membrane

Flashing membrane

Cap sheet or finished roofing layer

The exact system depends on the existing building, manufacturer, roof slope, substrate, drainage design, and project requirements. Flat Roof Drainage Is Critical Flat roofs are not usually completely flat.

They need sufficient drainage to move rainwater toward:

Roof drains

Scuppers

Gutters

Downspouts

If the existing roof has significant ponding, the drainage condition should be evaluated before installing the new roof.

Simply installing new roofing material over an area with a major drainage problem may not correct the underlying condition.

Depending on the roof, correcting drainage may involve:

Tapered materials

Crickets

Framing modifications

New sheathing

Drain modifications

Slope improvements

  • ●Every property should be evaluated individually.
  • ●Asphalt Shingle Roof Replacement in San Francisco
  • ●Many pitched San Francisco homes use architectural asphalt shingles.

Modern architectural shingles offer homeowners numerous advantages:

Attractive appearance

Multiple colors and styles

Good weather resistance

Relatively efficient installation

Manufacturer warranty options

Compatibility with many San Francisco architectural styles

Premium shingles may also provide enhanced features such as solar-reflective technology or upgraded manufacturer warranty eligibility.

The quality of installation, however, is just as important as the shingle itself.

A premium roofing product installed over deteriorated plywood or improperly installed flashing may still develop problems. What Is Included in a Complete Roof Replacement? A professional San Francisco roof replacement should address the complete roofing assembly.

Here is what homeowners should expect. 1. Existing Roof Removal Existing roofing materials are removed from the areas included in the project.

Depending on the roof, tear-off can include:

Shingles

Modified bitumen

Underlayment

Existing fasteners

Flashing

Roofing accessories

Deteriorated membranes

Removing the existing roofing allows the contractor to see what is underneath. 2. Roof Deck Inspection Once the old roof is removed, the exposed roof deck should be inspected.

Roof sheathing may contain damage that was impossible to identify during the original exterior inspection.

Common concealed problems include:

Rotten plywood

Delamination

Dry rot

Water-damaged sheathing

Previous improper repairs

Damaged framing

Structural deficiencies

Damaged decking should be properly addressed before the new roofing system is installed. 3. Roof Deck Repairs If deteriorated sheathing is discovered, damaged sections may need replacement.

This is one reason homeowners should ask their contractor before signing a contract:

How much does additional plywood cost if damaged decking is discovered?

A professional roofing proposal should explain how concealed damage will be handled. 4. Underlayment Underlayment provides an additional protective layer between the roof deck and finished roofing material.

Depending on the roofing system, this may include modern synthetic roofing underlayment or another manufacturer-approved product.

Underlayment plays an important part in the complete roofing system. 5. Waterproofing Critical Areas Additional waterproofing protection may be installed around vulnerable roof areas including:

Valleys

Roof penetrations

Skylights

Eaves

Chimneys

Roof-to-wall connections

Low-slope transitions

These are often the places where leaks develop first. 6. Flashing Installation Flashing is one of the most important components of a roof.

Flashing directs water away from joints and transitions where roofing materials meet another surface.

San Francisco homes can have complicated flashing conditions involving:

Parapet walls

Chimneys

Skylights

Neighboring walls

Plumbing vents

HVAC penetrations

Roof-to-wall transitions

Sidewalls

Headwalls

Poor flashing installation can cause leakage even when the main roofing material remains in excellent condition. 7. New Roofing Installation After the roof deck and waterproofing details are prepared, the new roofing system is installed according to the manufacturer's applicable installation instructions.

Depending on the property, this may include:

Architectural shingles

Modified bitumen roofing

Low-slope membranes

Combination roofing systems

8. Roof Penetrations Every penetration through the roof needs proper integration with the roofing system.

Examples include:

Plumbing vents

Exhaust vents

Skylights

Chimneys

Roof drains

HVAC equipment

Electrical penetrations

Solar-related components

These areas should be carefully inspected during reroofing. 9. Cleanup Roof replacement produces a significant amount of debris.

Professional cleanup should include removing roofing debris and checking work areas around the property.

Because San Francisco properties frequently have narrow side yards and neighboring buildings, debris management is especially important. 10. Final Inspection Once construction is complete, the contractor should review the finished roofing installation.

Projects requiring a building permit must also complete the applicable City inspection process.

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection allows reroofing inspections associated with permits to be requested through PermitSF. Do You Need a Permit for Roof Replacement in San Francisco? This is an important question.

Under San Francisco's current Building Code, reroofing can be exempt from a building permit when no roof sheathing is installed, repaired, or removed and the reroofed area does not exceed 25% of the entire roof surface within a 12-month period.

That means a typical full roof replacement in San Francisco generally requires a building permit.

Permit requirements can become more complicated when work involves:

Roof sheathing

Structural alterations

Historic properties

Major roof modifications

Rooftop equipment

Changes to drainage

Solar systems

Fire-safety considerations

Other exterior alterations

Certain historic buildings may also require additional Planning review when the roofing project changes the roof structure or sheathing.

Your roofing contractor should evaluate the permit requirements before beginning work. San Francisco Cool Roof Requirements Roof replacement in San Francisco may also involve applicable California energy-efficiency and cool roof requirements.

The exact requirements can depend on factors including:

Roof slope

Roofing area

Building type

Scope of construction

Roofing product

Applicable energy-code provisions

For applicable projects, roofing materials may need to meet specified solar reflectance and thermal emittance requirements.

Because codes change over time and different buildings can qualify for different exceptions or requirements, the roofing system should be confirmed for the particular property before installation. Roof Replacement on Historic San Francisco Homes San Francisco contains thousands of older and architecturally distinctive buildings.

If you own a Victorian, Edwardian, or historically significant home, roofing work may require additional planning.

A historic roof can include:

Unusual roof slopes

Decorative architectural elements

Turrets

Dormers

Custom gutters

Historic properties can also be subject to additional review depending on the scope of exterior alterations.

This is another reason why homeowners should avoid assuming that every San Francisco reroof is a simple one- or two-day shingle installation. Should You Replace Skylights During Roof Replacement? Roof replacement is one of the best times to evaluate existing skylights.

Removing an old roof provides access to skylight flashing, curbs, surrounding framing, and decking.

Older skylights may have:

Deteriorated seals

Damaged glazing

Corroded metal

Failed flashing

Rotten curbs

Deteriorated surrounding plywood

Previous patches

Interior water staining

If a skylight is already near the end of its useful life, replacing it at the same time as the roof can be more efficient than disturbing a newly installed roofing system several years later.

San Francisco also has many custom skylights that cannot always be replaced with a standard off-the-shelf unit.

In these cases, measurements and product availability should be confirmed before the roof replacement begins. What About Roof-to-Wall and Neighboring Roof Connections? This issue is particularly important in San Francisco.

Homes are frequently constructed extremely close together, and some roof areas connect directly against neighboring walls or roofs.

These areas can create complicated waterproofing conditions.

A contractor should evaluate:

Existing wall flashing

Counterflashing

Siding condition

Neighboring roofing

Parapet walls

Drainage

Property access

Waterproofing transitions

When work requires access to a neighboring property, the homeowner should determine whether permission is required before construction begins.

Contractors should avoid exposing more of the roof than can be properly protected when rain is expected.

San Francisco roofing schedules may change during periods of wet weather.

Homeowners should understand that a weather-related schedule adjustment is often made to protect the property rather than simply to delay the project. Roof Repair vs. Full Roof Replacement Sometimes a roof repair is enough. Roof Repair May Make Sense When:

A typical complete reroof generally requires a building permit. Under the San Francisco Building Code, limited reroofing not exceeding 25% of the roof surface within a 12-month period can qualify for a permit exemption when no roof sheathing is installed, repaired, or removed. What type of roof is best for San Francisco?

It depends on the building and roof slope. Architectural asphalt shingles are commonly used on pitched roofs, while modified bitumen and other low-slope systems are commonly used on flat and low-slope roofs. How much does a new roof cost in San Francisco?

Pricing depends on roof size, roofing material, height, access, complexity, number of existing layers, decking condition, skylights, flashing, permits, and other project conditions. An on-site inspection is the best way to obtain accurate pricing. How long does a roof replacement take?

Many residential roofing projects can be completed within several working days. Large or complicated properties, substantial dry rot, weather delays, specialty materials, skylights, or structural repairs may extend the schedule. Can you replace a flat roof in San Francisco?

Yes. Flat and low-slope roofing is common in San Francisco. The correct roofing system should be selected based on roof slope, drainage, existing conditions, and building requirements.

Should I replace my skylights when replacing my roof? Older skylights should be evaluated during reroofing. If a skylight is already deteriorated or approaching the end of its useful life, replacing it during roof construction may be more practical than disturbing a new roof later.

Can you install a new roof over my existing roof? Whether an overlay is appropriate depends on existing roofing layers, roof conditions, applicable codes, manufacturer requirements, weight, drainage, and the roofing system being installed. Does San Francisco require roof inspections after replacement? Permitted projects are subject to applicable City inspection requirements. San Francisco DBI currently allows reroofing inspections for PermitSF-issued permits to be requested through the PermitSF dashboard.
