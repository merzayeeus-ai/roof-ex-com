License Verification & CSLB

Hiring the right roofing contractor is arguably more important than choosing the right materials—even the best shingles or membrane systems will fail prematurely if installed incorrectly. In the Bay Area, where roofing projects routinely cost $15,000 to $45,000, vetting your contractor thoroughly protects both your investment and your home. California law requires that any contractor performing roofing work valued at $500 or more hold a valid license from the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). Specifically, roofing contractors must hold a C-39 Roofing license, which demonstrates specialized training and experience in roof installation, repair, and waterproofing. Always verify a contractor's license status directly on the CSLB website (cslb.ca.gov) before signing any agreement.

Insurance Requirements

Insurance verification is a non-negotiable step that many homeowners overlook. Your roofing contractor must carry both general liability insurance (minimum $1 million per occurrence is standard in the Bay Area) and workers' compensation insurance covering every crew member on your property. Request current certificates of insurance directly from the contractor and verify them with the issuing insurance company—not just by looking at a document the contractor hands you. If a contractor is uninsured or underinsured and a worker is injured on your property, you as the homeowner could be held financially liable. Similarly, if an uninsured contractor damages your property or a neighbor's property, you have no recourse beyond a lawsuit against the contractor personally.

Reading & Comparing Estimates

Learning how to read and compare roofing estimates is essential to making an informed decision. A professional estimate should include a detailed line-item breakdown covering: tear-off and disposal of existing roofing, new underlayment type and specification, primary roofing material with manufacturer and product name, flashing replacement details, ventilation improvements, drip edge and starter strip, permit fees, and cleanup and debris removal. Vague estimates that list only a lump-sum price or generic descriptions like 'new roof installed' are a major red flag. When comparing estimates from multiple contractors, make sure you're comparing equivalent scopes of work—a lower price often means a thinner scope, cheaper materials, or corners being cut on critical details like ice-and-water shield and step flashing.

Red Flags to Watch For

Several red flags should immediately disqualify a roofing contractor from consideration. Door-to-door solicitation following storms is a hallmark of fly-by-night operators who chase weather events and disappear after collecting payment. Demanding cash-only payment or a large upfront deposit (more than 10 percent or $1,000, whichever is less—per California law) is both illegal and a sign of financial instability. Contractors who refuse to provide a written contract, who pressure you to sign immediately, or who offer suspiciously low prices compared to other bids are almost always cutting corners. Legitimate contractors welcome questions, provide references willingly, and never pressure homeowners into rushed decisions.

Manufacturer Certifications

Manufacturer certifications can affect which warranty programs a project may qualify for, but they do not replace the product warranty document or written contract. Selected roofing systems may include up to 50 years of manufacturer/material coverage, while ROOF EXPRESS provides a separate 25-year workmanship warranty for qualifying new installations. ROOF EXPRESS currently lists GAF Certified Plus, CertainTeed ShingleMaster PRO, and Owens Corning Preferred Contractor credentials. Verify any contractor's current standing directly with the manufacturer, then request the exact warranty document, eligibility requirements, exclusions, registration steps, and workmanship terms before signing. Diamond Certified contractors have been vetted through an independent research process that includes customer surveys and credential verification.

Getting Bids & Checking References

Getting three to four written estimates from licensed, insured contractors is the standard recommendation for Bay Area homeowners planning a roofing project. Request references from each contractor—specifically recent projects in your city or neighborhood—and actually call those references to ask about communication, timeline accuracy, cleanup quality, and whether any issues arose after completion. Check online reviews on Google, Yelp, and the Better Business Bureau, but weigh patterns rather than individual reviews. A contractor with hundreds of reviews averaging 4.5+ stars is far more reliable than one with ten perfect 5-star reviews. Finally, understand your warranty documents: a standard manufacturer warranty may cover material defects, while an extended or workmanship program may cover additional items only when its product, installer, registration, and eligibility requirements are met. Compare the exact written terms rather than relying on a year count in an advertisement.

Key takeaways

  • Always verify your contractor's C-39 Roofing license on the CSLB website before signing any agreement
  • Demand proof of general liability insurance ($1M minimum) and workers' compensation coverage
  • Red flags include cash-only payment, door-to-door solicitation, no written contract, and large upfront deposits
  • Current manufacturer credentials and Diamond Certified status can be useful signals, but verify them and read the written warranty terms
