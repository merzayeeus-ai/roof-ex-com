Why Permits Are Required

Every roofing project in the Bay Area requires some form of building permit, whether you're replacing a few squares of shingles or installing an entirely new roof system. California Building Code mandates permits for any roofing work that involves removal and replacement of roofing materials, and Bay Area jurisdictions enforce this requirement consistently. The permit ensures that work is performed to code, that proper materials are used, and that a city inspector verifies the installation before the project is considered complete. Skipping permits might save a few hundred dollars upfront, but it creates serious problems—unpermitted work can void your homeowner's insurance, complicate home sales, and leave you liable for code violations.

Costs & Timelines by City

Permit costs and processing times vary significantly across Bay Area cities. San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection charges $300 to $600 for a standard residential re-roofing permit, with processing times of 1 to 3 weeks for over-the-counter permits and 4 to 8 weeks for projects requiring plan review. San Jose's permit fees run $250 to $500 with faster processing, typically 3 to 10 business days. Oakland charges $200 to $450 and processes most re-roofing permits within 1 to 2 weeks. Smaller Peninsula cities like San Mateo, Redwood City, and Palo Alto generally charge $150 to $400 and process permits within 5 to 10 business days. These are baseline ranges—projects involving structural changes, skylights, or solar panel integration require additional fees and longer review periods.

Re-Roofing vs Full Building Permits

The distinction between a simple re-roofing permit and a full building permit is important. A re-roofing permit (sometimes called a 'like-for-like' permit) applies when you're replacing the existing roof with the same type of material and making no structural changes. This is the fastest and least expensive permit type—often available over the counter with same-day or next-day approval. A full building permit is required when the project involves structural modifications such as adding skylights, changing the roof pitch, modifying the drainage system, adding a roof deck, or converting from one roof type to another (for example, shingle to tile, which adds significant weight). Full permits require engineered plans, structural calculations, and plan check review, adding weeks and $500 to $2,000+ to the permit costs.

Title 24 Compliance

Title 24 compliance is a mandatory component of every Bay Area roofing permit. California's Title 24 Building Energy Efficiency Standards require that roofing materials meet specific solar reflectance and thermal emittance values (cool roof requirements). For steep-slope residential roofs, this means selecting shingles or tiles that meet the minimum aged solar reflectance of 0.20 and thermal emittance of 0.75. For low-slope roofs, the requirements are more stringent. Your permit application must specify the roofing material and its Title 24 compliance documentation. Most major shingle manufacturers (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed) offer compliant products in their standard lines, so this requirement rarely limits your material choices but must be properly documented on the permit application.

The Inspection Process

The inspection process is the final step in closing your permit. After your roofing contractor completes the installation, a city building inspector visits the site to verify that the work matches the permitted scope and meets code requirements. Inspectors check proper underlayment installation, flashing details, ventilation adequacy, material compliance with the approved permit, and fire-resistance ratings. Most inspections are scheduled within 3 to 7 business days of the request. If the inspector finds deficiencies, a correction notice is issued and the contractor must address the items before requesting a re-inspection. A reputable contractor handles the entire permit and inspection process—from application to final sign-off—as part of their standard service.

What Your Contractor Should Handle

Your roofing contractor should manage the entire permitting process as part of their scope of work. This includes preparing the permit application, providing material specifications and Title 24 documentation, paying permit fees (which are then included in your project cost), scheduling inspections, and addressing any inspector corrections. If a contractor asks you to pull your own permit, that's a significant red flag—it often means they're unlicensed or trying to avoid accountability for code compliance. Licensed California C-39 roofing contractors are authorized to pull permits on your behalf, and doing so is standard practice throughout the Bay Area. Always verify your contractor's license number through the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB) before signing any contract.

Key takeaways

  • All Bay Area roofing projects require building permits—skipping them voids insurance and complicates home sales
  • Re-roofing permits cost $150–$600 depending on the city and project scope
  • Title 24 cool roof compliance documentation is required on every permit application
  • Your licensed contractor should handle the entire permit and inspection process for you
