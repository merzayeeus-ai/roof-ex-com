Why Velux Flashing Kits Matter

Velux is the leading skylight manufacturer in North America, and their engineered flashing kits are specifically designed to integrate with their skylight frames. Using the correct flashing kit is essential for leak-free performance.

Flashing Kit Types

Velux flashing kit types: EDL (for flat/profile roofing like asphalt shingles)—the most common type for Bay Area residential applications. EDP (for flat roofing materials)—used for TPO, modified bitumen, and built-up roofing. EDW (for wood shake/shingle roofing). ECL (for curb-mount skylights on flat roofs).

Selecting the Right Kit

Selecting the right kit: Match the kit to your roofing material AND skylight model/size. Velux uses a sizing system (e.g., C04, M06, S06) that corresponds to specific frame dimensions. The flashing kit model must match both the material type and the skylight size code.

Installation Best Practices

Installation best practices: Install flashing kit components in the correct sequence (bottom apron first, then side pieces, then top). Each component overlaps the one below it for proper water shedding. Integrate step flashing with surrounding shingle courses. Never use sealant as a substitute for proper flashing overlap—sealant is a supplement, not a primary waterproofing method.

Common Installation Mistakes

Common mistakes that cause leaks: using generic or field-fabricated flashing instead of manufacturer kits, installing components out of sequence, failing to integrate with the surrounding roofing material properly, over-relying on sealant instead of mechanical weatherproofing, and not extending ice and water shield underlayment around the skylight opening.

Flashing & Energy Performance

Proper flashing directly impacts the energy efficiency of your skylight installation. Even the highest-performing skylight unit (Velux models achieve U-values of 0.28–0.43 with SHGC ratings of 0.21–0.39) will underperform if flashing allows air infiltration or water intrusion. ENERGY STAR-qualified skylights require a U-factor of 0.55 or lower and SHGC of 0.30 or lower in our Northern California climate zone, but these ratings assume proper installation with manufacturer-specified flashing. A well-flashed skylight reduces lighting energy costs by 30–80% in the room served while maintaining the thermal envelope required by California Title 24 energy code. Title 24 treats skylights as fenestration, and the overall glazing budget for the home depends on each unit performing to its rated specifications.

Drawbacks of Improper Flashing

Improper flashing is the primary cause of skylight drawbacks that homeowners experience. Leaks from poorly installed flashing are the number one skylight complaint—manufacturer-engineered kits like the Velux EDL series virtually eliminate this risk when installed correctly. Without proper flashing, moisture intrusion can lead to condensation and ice formation in the skylight well, particularly in Bay Area microclimate zones like fog-prone Daly City or coastal Half Moon Bay. Water damage from flashing failures can also cause mold growth and structural deterioration in the surrounding roof framing. Rain noise, while inherent to skylights, is worsened by loose or improperly sealed flashing components—ensuring tight installation reduces vibration and sound transmission.

Code Compliance & Permit Requirements

Flashing kit installation must comply with Bay Area building codes and permit requirements. California Residential Code Section R308.6 requires tempered or laminated safety glass for skylight glazing, and inspectors will also verify that flashing is installed per manufacturer specifications during the permit inspection. Most Bay Area cities—including San Francisco, Oakland, San Mateo, Mountain View, and Walnut Creek—require a building permit for skylight installation that covers both the unit and the flashing system. OSHA and Cal/OSHA fall-through protection requirements apply during installation, particularly on commercial or multi-story buildings. Proper documentation of the flashing kit model and installation sequence is typically required for permit sign-off and is essential for maintaining the manufacturer warranty.

Key takeaways

  • Always use Velux-specific flashing kits matched to both roofing material and skylight size
  • EDL kits are most common for Bay Area residential asphalt shingle roofs
  • Install components in correct sequence: bottom apron, sides, then top
  • Generic or field-fabricated flashing is the leading cause of skylight leaks
  • Proper flashing preserves rated U-values (0.28–0.43) and Title 24 compliance
  • Most Bay Area cities require permits covering both skylight unit and flashing system
  • Improper flashing causes leaks, condensation, mold, and voided manufacturer warranties
