Why Spring 2026 Inspection Matters

Spring 2026 is one of the most important roof inspection windows in recent Bay Area memory. The 2025-2026 winter brought above-average rainfall across most of the Bay Area, with a series of atmospheric river events in January and February that stress-tested every residential and commercial roof in the region. Even roofs that performed without active leaks during the storms often suffered subtle damage—lifted flashing, displaced shingle granules, micro-cracks in sealants, ponding-induced membrane stress on flat roofs—that becomes problematic during next winter's storms if not addressed now. This checklist walks through everything to inspect on your roof this spring, what needs immediate attention, and what can wait until the next planned maintenance cycle.

Ground-Level Exterior Inspection Checklist

Start your spring 2026 inspection from the ground with a careful walk-around of your home. Look at your roof from multiple angles using binoculars if necessary—you don't need to climb on the roof to spot most issues. From the ground, check for: missing or visibly displaced shingles (especially along ridges, hips, and rake edges where wind uplift is highest); shingle granules accumulated in gutters, downspouts, or in driveway runoff areas (some granule loss is normal, but heavy accumulation indicates aging shingles); shiny metal flashing visible at chimneys, walls, and around vent pipes (shiny means the protective coating has weathered off and rust is starting); sagging gutters or downspouts pulled away from the fascia; visible daylight through soffit vents (good ventilation); and dark streaks running down siding from the roofline (algae or moisture migration). Document anything questionable with photos—your roofer will appreciate seeing the conditions before climbing up.

Attic & Interior Inspection Checklist

Interior inspection is equally important and often reveals problems before they're visible from outside. In your attic (use a flashlight and check on a sunny day for best visibility), look for: water staining on rafters, decking, or insulation (any moisture sign is significant—even 'old' stains may indicate active or recurrent leaks); daylight visible at unexpected locations (around chimneys, vent pipes, or wall junctions—not the soffit vents which should let light through); damp or matted insulation; mold or mildew odors; rusted nail tips coming through from above (indicates condensation issues from inadequate ventilation); animal evidence (droppings, nests, chewed wood—rodents can enter through small roof gaps and damage wiring). In living spaces below the roof, inspect ceilings for new water stains, peeling paint near the ceiling line, or bubbling drywall texture—any of these signal water migration through the roof system.

Flat Roof Specific Inspection Checklist

Flat roofs require their own dedicated inspection after winter storms because they fail differently than pitched roofs. Walk the flat roof carefully (or have your contractor do this) and look for: ponding water that hasn't drained within 48 hours of rain (indicates drainage failure or membrane settling); blistered or bubbled membrane sections (water trapped beneath the membrane); seam separation at panel edges or around penetrations; cracked or split sealants around drains, vents, and curbs; punctures from fallen tree debris (winter storms commonly drop branches that puncture TPO and modified bitumen membranes); cap nails or fasteners working loose; and ballast displacement on gravel-ballasted systems. The most critical spring-fix items on flat roofs are seam re-sealing and drain clearing—both are affordable maintenance items that prevent the catastrophic failures (interior flooding, structural damage) that occur when small issues go unaddressed.

Urgent Issues to Fix Before Next Winter

Some spring 2026 findings need immediate attention before the next rainy season. Critical fixes you should not delay: any active leak (even slow drips); missing shingles in fields larger than two adjacent shingles; flashing visibly pulled away from chimneys, walls, or skylights; ponding water on flat roofs that hasn't drained; mold growth in the attic; cracked or broken roof tiles that expose underlayment; gutters and downspouts that are detached or non-functional; ridge vents or soffit vents that are blocked or damaged. These items pose immediate risk during the next storm or are likely to worsen rapidly without intervention. Cost-wise, small repairs handled in spring (typically $400 to $2,500) consistently prevent major repairs that would otherwise be needed in fall ($3,000 to $15,000+). The math overwhelmingly favors proactive spring repair over reactive fall emergency response.

Findings That Can Wait Until Next Year

Other findings can typically wait until your normal maintenance window or next planned project. Items that are not urgent: light surface granule loss without bare spots; minor algae streaking on north-facing slopes (treatable with zinc strips during planned maintenance); slightly weathered sealants that are still intact; minor gutter sagging without functional impact; small dents in metal flashing without water-tightness issues; and aesthetic issues like color fading or minor staining. Schedule these for your next maintenance visit (typically annually or every other year) rather than emergency service. If your roof is approaching the 18 to 22 year mark and shows multiple minor issues plus any major issue, this is the spring to start getting replacement quotes—replacing in summer 2026 saves money compared to emergency replacement after a future storm and lets you choose materials and timing strategically. Key takeaways

  • Spring 2026 inspections are critical after the heavy 2025-2026 winter storms — subtle damage from atmospheric rivers should be addressed before next winter
  • Ground-level inspection (binoculars, no climbing required) catches most exterior issues — focus on shingle displacement, flashing condition, and gutter integrity
  • Attic inspection reveals leaks before they show in living areas — look for water staining, daylight, mold odors, and condensation evidence
  • Flat roof inspection priorities: ponding water, seam separation, sealant condition, and drain function
  • Critical fixes (active leaks, missing shingles, displaced flashing) should not wait — small repairs ($400-$2,500) prevent major repairs ($3,000-$15,000) later
