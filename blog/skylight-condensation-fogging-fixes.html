Understanding Skylight Moisture

Skylight condensation and fogging are common complaints in the Bay Area's humid coastal climate. Understanding the cause is essential for determining the right fix—solutions range from simple ventilation adjustments to full skylight replacement.

Interior Condensation Causes

Condensation on the interior glass surface (room side): This is typically a humidity and ventilation issue, not a skylight defect. Causes include high indoor humidity from cooking, bathing, or laundry, inadequate bathroom exhaust ventilation, and poor air circulation in the skylight well.

Between-Pane Fogging

Fogging between glass panes (inside the insulated glass unit): This indicates seal failure in the insulated glass unit (IGU). The desiccant between panes is saturated and can no longer absorb moisture. The glass unit needs replacement—this cannot be fixed with sealant or ventilation changes.

Fixing Interior Condensation

Solutions for interior condensation: reduce indoor humidity (target 30-50% relative humidity), ensure bathroom fans run 20+ minutes after showering, install a dehumidifier in humid seasons, improve air circulation with ceiling fans, and consider adding ventilation to the skylight shaft.

IGU Seal Failure Solutions

Solutions for IGU seal failure: Velux and other manufacturers often warranty the glass unit for 10-20 years. If under warranty, the glass unit can be replaced without replacing the entire skylight frame. If out of warranty, evaluate whether glass-only replacement or full skylight replacement makes more sense (older skylights may have multiple issues).

Recommended Skylight Glass

For Bay Area homes, we recommend dual-pane low-E glass skylights with argon gas fill for optimal condensation resistance and energy performance. Velux's FCM and FS models with ComfortPlus glass provide excellent performance in our climate.

Energy Efficiency & Title 24 Compliance

Energy efficiency is a key consideration when addressing condensation issues. Modern Velux skylights achieve U-values between 0.28 and 0.43, with Solar Heat Gain Coefficients (SHGC) ranging from 0.21 to 0.39 depending on glass type. ENERGY STAR-qualified skylights must meet a U-factor of 0.55 or lower and an SHGC of 0.30 or lower in our climate zone. Upgrading to a high-performance unit can reduce lighting costs by 30–80% in the room served while meeting California Title 24 energy code requirements for fenestration. Under Title 24, skylights count as glazing area and must comply with maximum U-factor and SHGC limits based on the total fenestration budget for the home.

Potential Drawbacks & Mitigation

Potential drawbacks to consider with skylights: south- and west-facing installations can cause excessive heat gain in summer months, which can be mitigated with Low-E glass coatings or integral blinds available on Velux solar-powered models. Rain noise can be noticeable on skylight glass—laminated glass options significantly reduce sound transmission. Condensation and ice formation can occur in Bay Area microclimate zones such as coastal Pacifica or foggy Daly City hilltops, making proper ventilation and glass selection even more critical. UV exposure through skylights can fade furnishings over time, though modern units include UV-blocking coatings that filter out 95%+ of harmful rays as standard.

Bay Area Building Code Requirements

Bay Area building codes impose specific requirements for skylight installations. California Residential Code Section R308.6 requires all skylight glazing to be tempered or laminated safety glass. OSHA and Cal/OSHA mandate fall-through protection for skylights on commercial buildings, and residential skylights must meet similar safety glazing standards. Most Bay Area cities—including San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose, Daly City, and Palo Alto—require a building permit for new skylight installations or replacements that alter the roof opening. If a skylight is installed in a bedroom, egress requirements under the California Residential Code may apply depending on the room's existing egress provisions.

Key takeaways

  • Interior condensation is usually a humidity/ventilation issue, not a skylight defect
  • Fogging between panes indicates IGU seal failure requiring glass unit replacement
  • Target 30–50% indoor relative humidity to minimize skylight condensation
  • Dual-pane low-E glass with argon fill provides best condensation resistance
  • Modern skylights achieve U-values of 0.28–0.43 and can reduce lighting costs 30–80%
  • California code requires tempered or laminated glass for all skylight installations
  • Most Bay Area cities require building permits for skylight work
