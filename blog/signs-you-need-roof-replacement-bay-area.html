Key Warning Signs Overview

Knowing when to replace your roof can save you thousands in water damage, structural repairs, and energy costs. Here are the key warning signs that indicate your roof has reached the end of its useful life.

Age as Primary Indicator

Age is the primary indicator. Most asphalt shingle roofs in the Bay Area last 20-30 years depending on quality and maintenance. If your roof is approaching or past this age range, replacement should be on your radar even if no visible damage is present.

Curling & Buckling Shingles

Curling or buckling shingles are a clear sign of aging. When shingles curl at the edges or buckle in the middle, they've lost their waterproofing effectiveness and are vulnerable to wind damage and leaks.

Granule Loss Detection

Excessive granule loss appears as bare spots on shingles and heavy granule accumulation in gutters. Granules protect the asphalt from UV degradation—without them, shingles deteriorate rapidly in California's strong sun.

Multiple Leaks & Repairs

Multiple leaks or repeated repairs suggest systemic failure rather than isolated damage. If you're calling a roofer every year or two for leak repairs, the cumulative cost of repairs will quickly exceed the cost of replacement.

Other Critical Indicators

Other indicators include: sagging roof deck (structural concern requiring immediate attention), moss or algae growth compromising shingle integrity, visible daylight through roof boards from the attic, and neighbors replacing their roofs (homes built around the same time often need replacement around the same time).

Day-by-Day Replacement Process

Once you've decided replacement is necessary, understanding the step-by-step process helps you prepare. A typical Bay Area residential roof replacement follows a consistent day-by-day timeline. Day 1 focuses entirely on tear-off: a crew of 4-8 workers arrives between 7:00 and 8:00 AM, lays protective tarps over landscaping, decks, and driveways, and removes all existing roofing material down to the deck sheathing. Dump trailers are positioned for efficient debris removal, and by end of day the entire roof deck is exposed and inspected for rot or damage. Any compromised sheathing is replaced before proceeding—this is common in Bay Area homes over 30 years old, especially in fog belt cities like Daly City, Pacifica, and the Sunset District where moisture intrusion is prevalent.

Installation & Materials Phase

Days 2-3 are dedicated to installation. The crew applies ice-and-water shield membrane in all valleys, around penetrations, and along eaves, followed by synthetic underlayment across the entire roof surface. New drip edge, starter strips, and step flashing are installed before shingle courses begin from the eaves upward. Proper ventilation components—ridge vents, soffit vents, or both—are installed or upgraded during this phase. For a standard 2,000-square-foot Bay Area home, installation typically requires 1.5 to 2 full days depending on roof complexity, number of valleys, and penetrations like skylights or plumbing vents.

Cleanup, Pets & Weather Delays

Day 4 covers final cleanup, quality inspection, and debris removal. The crew conducts a thorough magnetic sweep of the property to collect any stray nails, removes all protective coverings from landscaping and hardscaping, and hauls away remaining debris. Before any project begins, homeowners should move vehicles out of the driveway and at least 15 feet from the house, secure outdoor furniture and patio items, and make arrangements for pets—dogs should stay indoors or at a boarding facility during tear-off day, as the noise and falling debris can be stressful and dangerous. In the Bay Area's rainy season (November through March), ROOF EXPRESS monitors weather forecasts 72 hours in advance and applies waterproof tarping between work phases if rain is expected, ensuring your home stays protected even if the project spans a weather delay of several days.

Key takeaways

  • Most Bay Area asphalt shingle roofs last 20–30 years before needing replacement
  • Curling, buckling shingles and excessive granule loss are clear aging signs
  • Repeated leak repairs signal systemic failure—replacement becomes more cost-effective
  • A sagging roof deck is a structural concern requiring immediate attention
  • A typical replacement takes 3-4 days: Day 1 tear-off, Days 2-3 installation, Day 4 cleanup
  • Crews of 4-8 workers protect landscaping, vehicles, and pets throughout the process
  • During rainy season, waterproof tarping is applied between work phases to protect exposed decking
