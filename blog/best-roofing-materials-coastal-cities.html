Coastal Roofing Challenges

Coastal Bay Area cities—San Francisco, Pacifica, Daly City, Half Moon Bay, and others along the fog belt—present unique challenges for roofing materials. Persistent moisture, salt air, and wind exposure require materials that can handle these conditions.

Asphalt Shingles in Coastal Zones

Asphalt shingles in coastal zones: Standard shingles perform adequately but may have slightly shorter lifespans due to constant moisture exposure. Choose algae-resistant shingles (AR designation) to prevent dark streaking common in fog-belt areas. Premium architectural shingles with enhanced wind ratings (130+ mph) are recommended for exposed hilltop locations.

Metal Roofing for Coast

Metal roofing for coastal homes: Standing seam metal is excellent for coastal environments—it's impervious to moisture, resists wind uplift, and won't support algae growth. However, use aluminum or properly coated steel to prevent salt air corrosion. Copper develops a protective patina and is the premium coastal choice. Avoid exposed fastener metal panels in high-wind coastal areas.

Tile Roofing Performance

Tile roofing: Concrete and clay tiles perform extremely well in coastal zones. Their mass resists wind, they don't absorb moisture, and they're unaffected by salt air. The main consideration is weight—ensure your structure can support tile roofing (900-1,100 lbs per square vs 250-300 for shingles).

Flat Roofing Considerations

Flat roofing in coastal areas: TPO and PVC membranes resist moisture and UV degradation well. Modified bitumen performs adequately but requires more frequent maintenance in high-moisture environments. Ensure excellent drainage on coastal flat roofs—persistent fog moisture can contribute to ponding if drainage is marginal.

Our Coastal Recommendations

Our coastal recommendation hierarchy: 1) Standing seam metal (best long-term value for exposed locations), 2) Premium architectural shingles with AR treatment (best value for most coastal homes), 3) Concrete tile (excellent durability, if structure supports it), 4) TPO membrane (for flat sections). We always recommend enhanced underlayment and ice and water shield in all valleys and penetrations for coastal installations.

Key takeaways

  • Coastal homes face persistent moisture, salt air, and wind requiring specialized materials
  • Standing seam metal is the top choice for exposed coastal locations
  • Use algae-resistant (AR) shingles in fog-belt areas to prevent dark streaking
  • Enhanced underlayment and ice & water shield are essential for all coastal installations
