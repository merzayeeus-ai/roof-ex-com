Valley Flashing Open Vs Closed

Open valley vs. closed valley flashing: pros, cons, costs, and which method works best for Bay Area roofs.

Understanding Roof Valleys

Roof valleys—where two roof planes meet—handle more water than any other part of your roof. The flashing method used in these critical areas significantly impacts both performance and longevity.

Open Metal Valley Design

Open metal valleys use a continuous strip of metal (typically aluminum or galvanized steel) visible between the shingle courses on either side. Water flows directly on the metal surface, providing excellent drainage and easy debris clearing.

Closed-Cut Valley Method

Closed-cut valleys (also called California-cut) run shingles from one roof plane across the valley, then cut shingles from the other plane are laid on top with a clean cut line. No metal is visible, creating a cleaner aesthetic.

Bay Area Recommendations

For Bay Area conditions, we generally recommend open metal valleys for several reasons: superior water handling capacity during heavy rain events, easier inspection and maintenance, longer lifespan than shingle-covered valleys, better performance with debris (leaves and pine needles slide off metal more easily), and simpler repair if issues develop.

When Closed-Cut Works

Closed-cut valleys have their place—they provide a cleaner look on high-visibility roof sections and work well on lower-volume water flow areas. Some homeowners in upscale neighborhoods prefer the seamless appearance.

Woven Valleys Overview

Woven valleys (alternating shingle courses across the valley) are a third option but are generally not recommended for Bay Area applications due to their lower water handling capacity and higher maintenance requirements.

Key takeaways

  • Roof valleys handle more water than any other roof area
  • Open metal valleys offer superior drainage and easier maintenance for Bay Area conditions
  • Closed-cut valleys provide cleaner aesthetics but lower water capacity
  • Woven valleys are generally not recommended for Bay Area applications
