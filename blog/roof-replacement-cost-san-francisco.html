San Francisco Cost Overview

There is no defensible one-size-fits-all price for a San Francisco roof replacement. A useful budget starts with a site-specific, written scope rather than a neighborhood price table. The variables to identify near the top of any estimate are measured roof size and geometry, material or roof system, access and staging, tear-off and deck condition, the permit and inspection path, and explicit exclusions.

Six Variables That Change a Quote

Roof size is more than the home's floor area: the estimate should identify the roof planes or measured design area, pitch, valleys, penetrations, edges, and any low-slope sections. Material changes the installation system and details. Steep or complex geometry, limited access, street staging, and protection of adjacent property can change labor and logistics. These are reasons to compare scopes—not reasons to assume a citywide price.

Materials, Tear-Off & Access

Ask for line items that separate new roofing material and underlayment from tear-off, disposal, flashing, ventilation, deck or sheathing repairs, access or traffic control, permit handling, inspections, cleanup, and taxes or fees. The estimate should say how hidden conditions will be documented and approved before extra work proceeds. It should also identify what is not included, such as solar or skylight work, structural changes, interior repairs, or owner-provided items.

Permits & City Requirements

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection says a permit is required before installing, repairing, or removing roofing material, subject to the exemptions in San Francisco Building Code Section 106A.2. Its online reroofing route is limited to work that does not require plans or replacement of structural components such as framing or decking/sheathing. DBI also asks applicants for the existing and proposed material, whether the roof is flat or pitched, visible changes, street space, and project valuation. Confirm the current path with DBI; do not rely on an old fee or timeline in a generic cost guide.

Exclusions to Clarify

Permit and access questions belong in the written scope. Confirm who submits the application, schedules inspections, coordinates any required street space, and supplies material or planning information. Work that involves plans, structural components, or visible changes may follow a different review path than no-plans reroofing, so the permit allowance should not be treated as a universal San Francisco charge.

Repair vs. Full Replacement

Repair versus replacement is an inspection decision, not a fixed percentage rule or a guaranteed resale return. Ask what evidence supports the recommendation: age and remaining service life, leak history, surface and flashing condition, deck or sheathing findings, and whether a repair can be warranted. Key Takeaways

  • San Francisco replacement pricing is scope-specific; request a measured, line-item estimate rather than a universal price
  • The main variables are roof size and geometry, material, access, tear-off and deck condition, permits, and exclusions
  • SF DBI says roofing permits are required before covered work, with listed code exemptions and different paths for plans or structural work
  • Clarify flashing, ventilation, deck repairs, staging, disposal, inspections, and exclusions before comparing estimates
  • Use the San Francisco roofing service page for local help and a site-specific estimate
